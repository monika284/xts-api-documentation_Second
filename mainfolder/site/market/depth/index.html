    Market Depth

    Market Depth provides real-time visibility into the full order book — all pending buy and sell orders at every price level. It shows market liquidity, supply and demand, and potential price pressure far beyond the last traded price.

    Trading Terminals Liquidity Analysis Support & Resistance Algorithmic Trading Order Flow Analysis

    What is Market Depth?

    The order book is a real-time list of every pending buy and sell order for an instrument. Market Depth shows you the top 5 levels on each side — price, quantity waiting at that price, and how many individual orders are queued there.

    Bids (Buy Orders)

    Pending orders from buyers. Listed from highest to lowest price. The top bid is the highest a buyer will pay right now.

    Buy Side

    Spread

    The gap between the best ask and the best bid. A smaller spread means more buyers and sellers are actively trading.

    Center Gap

    Asks (Sell Orders)

    Pending orders from sellers. Listed from lowest to highest price. The top ask is the minimum price a seller will accept.

    API Endpoint

    POST /apimarketdata/instruments/quotes xtsMessageCode: 1502

    Market Depth data uses the same quotes endpoint as touchline data — pass xtsMessageCode: 1502 to receive the 5-level order book instead of just LTP and OHLC.

    Reading Market Liquidity

    The depth and size of the order book tells you how easily you can execute a large order without moving the market price.

    High Liquidity Large quantities on both bid and ask sides. Tight spread. Orders fill quickly at the expected price. Ideal for large orders.
    Tight spread (₹0.05 – ₹0.20)
    High total bid/ask quantity
    Many orders per level
    Low Liquidity Thin order book with small quantities. Wide spread. Large orders can move the price significantly. Risky for big trades.
    Wide spread (₹1 – ₹5+)
    Low total bid/ask quantity
    Few orders per level

    Identifying Support and Resistance

    Large concentrations of orders at specific price levels can act as support (large buy orders) or resistance (large sell orders).

    Support Level
    A price where a significant number of buy orders are stacked. The market often bounces back up when it reaches this level because buyers absorb selling pressure.
    2965.30 — 1,800 qty
    2965.20 — 850 qty
    2965.00 — 12,000 qty ← Support
    Resistance Level
    A price where a significant number of sell orders are stacked. The market often struggles to break above this level because sellers keep absorbing buying pressure.
    2970.00 — 15,000 qty ← Resistance
    2969.50 — 600 qty
    2969.00 — 900 qty

    Data Flow

    1
    Client Application Sends instrument token with xtsMessageCode 1502
    2
    Market Depth API Routes to the exchange order book data feed
    3
    Exchange Order Book Real-time snapshot of top 5 bid and ask levels
    4
    Depth Response Bids[ ] and Asks[ ] arrays — price, size, order count
    5
    Trading Terminal Renders order book, spread, and liquidity visualization

    Best Practices

    Combine with Quote Data

    Use market depth alongside the last traded price and OHLC to get the complete market picture before placing an order.

    Check Liquidity Before Large Orders

    Before placing a large order, verify there is enough quantity at the top levels. Thin books can cause significant slippage.

    Use WebSocket for Live Updates

    Subscribe to event code 1502 via WebSocket instead of polling. Order books update many times per second.

    Watch for Order Book Imbalance

    A large imbalance in total bid vs ask quantity often signals short-term price direction — more bids = buy pressure, more asks = sell pressure.

    Common Errors

    404
    Instrument Not Found The instrument token does not exist in the specified exchange segment.
    401
    Unauthorized Market data token is missing, expired, or invalid.
    429
    Rate Limit Exceeded Too many depth requests. Use WebSocket streaming for continuous updates.
